CLIMATE AND BIODIVERSITY

Temperature and precipitation influence ecosystem productivity, water availability and species distributions.

Low rainfall can increase water stress for plants and organisms. Repeated drought conditions can reduce vegetation productivity and affect species survival.

Increasing temperatures can increase evaporative demand and water stress, particularly where rainfall is limited.

Climate stress can interact with habitat loss and land degradation. Species with limited habitat or movement options may have fewer opportunities to respond to changing environmental conditions.

Vegetation cover can influence soil temperature, evaporation and water retention.

Agroforestry and diversified vegetation can provide microclimatic variation and may reduce exposure to extreme environmental conditions in some agricultural systems.

Climate-related biodiversity impacts depend strongly on local climate, species characteristics, habitat availability and management.
