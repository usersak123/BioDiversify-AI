SOIL HEALTH AND BIODIVERSITY

Soil organic carbon is an important indicator of soil health. Organic carbon contributes to soil structure, nutrient cycling, water retention and biological activity.

Higher soil organic matter can provide habitat and energy sources for soil organisms and can improve soil structure and water holding capacity.

Soil biodiversity includes microorganisms, fungi, nematodes, earthworms and other organisms. These organisms contribute to decomposition, nutrient cycling and soil ecosystem functioning.

Soil moisture influences plant growth, microbial activity and survival of soil organisms. Low soil moisture can increase plant stress and constrain biological activity.

Soil pH influences nutrient availability and microbial communities. Extremely acidic or alkaline conditions can restrict the availability of some nutrients and affect biological processes.

Management practices such as maintaining soil cover, adding organic matter, reducing unnecessary soil disturbance and diversifying crops can influence soil biological processes.
