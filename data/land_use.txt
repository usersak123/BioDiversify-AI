LAND USE AND BIODIVERSITY

Land-use change is a major driver of biodiversity change.

Monoculture systems contain relatively limited crop diversity compared with diversified agricultural systems.

Crop diversification can increase vegetation structure and provide different ecological resources.

Intercropping involves growing multiple crop species together and can increase crop diversity within agricultural land.

Agroforestry integrates trees with crops or livestock. Trees can provide habitat, shade, organic matter and structural diversity.

Hedgerows, flowering strips, buffer zones and field margins can provide habitat and movement resources for insects, birds and other organisms.

Habitat connectivity is important because isolated habitat patches can restrict movement between populations.

The ecological effect of a land-use intervention depends on local climate, soil, crop selection, management intensity and landscape context.
