INTERVENTION: Cover Crops

Action:
Introduce non-crop vegetation such as legumes or grasses between main crop cycles.

Mechanism:
Cover crops maintain vegetation cover, reduce erosion, contribute organic residues, improve soil structure and can provide habitat and resources for soil organisms and insects.

Environmental metrics:
- Soil organic carbon
- Soil moisture
- Soil erosion
- Soil biological activity
- Habitat diversity
- Pollinator resources

Multi-metric relationship:
Increasing vegetation cover can protect soil from erosion and reduce direct soil exposure. Greater organic inputs can support soil biological activity, while additional vegetation can provide habitat resources.

Time horizon:
Short term: vegetation cover and erosion protection.
Medium term: changes in soil biological activity and organic matter.
Long term: potential improvement in soil health and habitat quality.

Evidence source:
FAO Global Soil Partnership resources and peer-reviewed research on soil organic matter and biodiversity.

Important limitation:
The magnitude of improvement depends on climate, species selected, management, soil type and duration.

INTERVENTION: Intercropping

Action:
Grow two or more compatible crop species together.

Mechanism:
Intercropping increases plant diversity and can create different vegetation structures and resource availability within agricultural land.

Environmental metrics:
- Crop diversity
- Habitat diversity
- Soil biological activity
- Pollinator resources
- Water-use efficiency

Multi-metric relationship:
Greater plant diversity can increase habitat complexity while interactions between crops can influence resource use and soil processes.

Time horizon:
Short to medium term.

Evidence source:
FAO biodiversity and sustainable agriculture resources; peer-reviewed research on crop diversification.

Important limitation:
Effects depend on crop combinations, climate, soil conditions and management.

--------------------------------------------------

INTERVENTION: Agroforestry

Action:
Integrate trees with crops or livestock.

Mechanism:
Trees can provide habitat, shade, root systems, litter inputs and structural diversity. They can also influence microclimate and water dynamics.

Environmental metrics:
- Habitat diversity
- Soil organic carbon
- Soil moisture
- Temperature exposure
- Species richness
- Landscape connectivity

Multi-metric relationship:
Trees can modify microclimate and provide additional habitat while contributing organic matter to soil. This can be particularly relevant where high temperature and water stress occur.

Time horizon:
Medium to long term.

Evidence source:
FAO agroforestry resources and peer-reviewed agroecology research.

Important limitation:
Tree species selection, water availability, spacing and competition with crops must be considered.

--------------------------------------------------

INTERVENTION: Flowering Field Margins

Action:
Create strips of flowering plants around or within agricultural fields.

Mechanism:
Flowering vegetation can provide nectar, pollen and refuge for pollinators and other beneficial organisms.

Environmental metrics:
- Pollinator abundance
- Habitat diversity
- Vegetation diversity
- Species richness

Multi-metric relationship:
Increasing habitat diversity can provide resources for insects while field margins can also reduce the ecological isolation of agricultural habitats.

Time horizon:
Short to medium term.

Evidence source:
FAO biodiversity resources and peer-reviewed research on ecological intensification.

--------------------------------------------------

INTERVENTION: Habitat Connectivity

Action:
Connect isolated habitat patches using hedgerows, vegetation corridors or restored field margins.

Mechanism:
Connectivity can facilitate movement between habitat patches and reduce isolation of populations.

Environmental metrics:
- Habitat connectivity
- Species movement
- Habitat diversity
- Species richness

Multi-metric relationship:
Land-use fragmentation can interact with climate stress because organisms may have fewer opportunities to move toward suitable conditions when habitats are isolated.

Time horizon:
Medium to long term.

Evidence source:
IPBES global biodiversity assessment and ecological research on habitat fragmentation.

--------------------------------------------------

INTERVENTION: Reduce Broad-Spectrum Pesticide Pressure

Action:
Reduce unnecessary broad-spectrum pesticide use and adopt integrated pest management where appropriate.

Mechanism:
Broad-spectrum pesticides can affect non-target organisms depending on chemical, dose, application and exposure.

Environmental metrics:
- Non-target organism pressure
- Pollinator risk
- Biodiversity
- Pest regulation

Multi-metric relationship:
Reducing unnecessary pesticide pressure can complement habitat restoration because organisms need both suitable habitat and reduced chemical pressure.

Time horizon:
Short to medium term.

Evidence source:
FAO integrated pest management resources and peer-reviewed research.

Important limitation:
Specific pesticide decisions require consideration of the crop, pest, chemical and local regulations.
