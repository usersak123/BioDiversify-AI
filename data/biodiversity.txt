BIODIVERSITY

Biodiversity can be described through multiple dimensions including species richness, species composition, habitat diversity and functional diversity.

Species richness refers to the number of different species present in an area.

Habitat diversity describes the variety of habitat types or ecological structures available to organisms.

Agricultural landscapes containing multiple vegetation types can provide more ecological niches than landscapes dominated by a single crop.

Flowering plants and diverse vegetation can provide food and habitat resources for pollinators and other organisms.

Habitat fragmentation can divide continuous habitat into smaller isolated patches. Fragmentation can affect movement, reproduction and access to resources for species.

Biodiversity contributes to ecosystem functions including pollination, nutrient cycling, decomposition and pest regulation.

Increasing habitat complexity and maintaining connected habitat patches can support biodiversity.
